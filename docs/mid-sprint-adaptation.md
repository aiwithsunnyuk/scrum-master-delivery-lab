# Mid-Sprint Adaptation

## Event

**Day 4:** Article-taxonomy service becomes unavailable due to an upstream production incident.

## Initial impact

US-005 — Filter results by category — cannot complete its integration path.

## Immediate Scrum Master questions

1. Does this threaten the Sprint Goal?
2. What work can continue?
3. Is there a safe alternative to waiting?
4. Who owns resolution of the dependency?
5. What does the Product Owner need to know?
6. What should be adapted in the Sprint Backlog?

## Options inspected

### Option A — Wait
Risk: idle capacity and reduced ability to achieve the Sprint Goal.

### Option B — Continue independent work
Preferred while dependency is unresolved.

### Option C — Build an unvalidated workaround
Rejected because it could create rework and quality risk.

## Decision

The team continues US-008 and US-012 while the dependency is actively escalated. The Product Owner is informed of the impact and trade-off.

When the service becomes available, Developers resume US-005 integration.

## Why this is Scrum Master evidence

The Scrum Master did not:

- Assign Developers to tasks.
- Decide product priority.
- Hide the problem.
- Promise a delivery date to stakeholders.
- Create a workaround without the Developers.

The Scrum Master did:

- Make the impediment transparent.
- Facilitate collaboration.
- Keep the Sprint Goal visible.
- Help the team adapt.
- Ensure the issue remained visible until resolved.

## Interview answer

**Question:** "What do you do when a major dependency blocks a Sprint?"

**Answer:** "First I make the impact transparent and bring the Developers and relevant owner together. I help the team inspect whether the Sprint Goal is threatened and what work can continue. I avoid assigning tasks or making product decisions for the team. If escalation is required, I facilitate it and keep the Product Owner informed. The goal is to remove the impediment while enabling the team to adapt its plan rather than simply waiting."
