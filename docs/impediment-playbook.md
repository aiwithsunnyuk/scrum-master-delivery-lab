# Impediment Management Playbook

## 1. Detect

An impediment is anything that materially prevents or threatens the team's ability to progress toward the Sprint Goal.

Sources include:

- Daily Scrum
- Developer collaboration
- Product Backlog refinement
- Stakeholder conversations
- Dependency reviews
- Quality/testing signals
- Retrospectives

## 2. Classify

### Critical
Immediate threat to Sprint Goal, release, security or production stability.

### High
Significant delivery impact requiring cross-team action or escalation.

### Medium
Slows progress but the team has a viable path around it.

### Low
Friction worth recording but not requiring immediate escalation.

## 3. Make transparent

Capture:

- What happened?
- Which work is affected?
- Impact?
- Owner?
- Date raised?
- Next action?
- Escalation path?
- Target resolution?

## 4. Facilitate ownership

The Scrum Master does not automatically become the owner.

Examples:

- Technical environment → Engineering owner
- Product decision → Product Owner
- External dependency → Dependency owner
- Process issue → Relevant delivery/system owner

## 5. Escalate based on impact and aging

Escalation should be purposeful, not a reflex.

Ask:

- Can the team resolve this itself?
- Is another team required?
- Has the impediment exceeded its agreed response window?
- Is the Sprint Goal threatened?
- Is there a customer, security or production impact?

## 6. Close properly

An item is not closed merely because someone says "fixed."

Validate:

- Resolution occurred.
- Affected work can progress.
- No important follow-up remains.
- Learning has been captured where useful.

## 7. Learn

Repeated impediments should trigger system-level improvement.

Example:

**Repeated environment access delays → improve access provisioning before Sprint Planning.**
