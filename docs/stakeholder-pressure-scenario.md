# Stakeholder Pressure Scenario

## Situation

On Day 6 of Sprint 05, a senior business stakeholder asks the team to add a "Popular Articles" feature immediately because a customer demonstration is approaching.

The feature is already in the Product Backlog but is not part of the current Sprint Goal.

## Risk

If the team accepts the request informally:

- Sprint focus may be disrupted.
- The Product Owner's ordering responsibility may be bypassed.
- Developers may receive conflicting priorities.
- The Sprint Goal could be threatened.

## Scrum Master response

### Step 1 — Listen

Understand why the stakeholder believes the request is urgent.

### Step 2 — Make the current commitment transparent

Show the Sprint Goal and current Sprint Backlog.

### Step 3 — Bring the Product Owner into the decision

The Scrum Master does not decide product priority.

### Step 4 — Inspect options

Possible options:

- Defer the request.
- Reorder future Product Backlog Items.
- If the Sprint Goal remains viable, collaborate with the Product Owner and Developers on an appropriate Sprint Backlog adaptation.
- If the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete, the Product Owner has the authority to cancel the Sprint.

### Step 5 — Close the communication loop

The stakeholder receives a clear explanation of the decision and its rationale.

## Example response

"I understand why the demonstration creates urgency. The current Sprint is focused on improving request-status visibility. Let's bring the Product Owner into the conversation so we can compare the value of this request with the current Product Goal and Sprint Goal. I can facilitate that discussion, but I won't create a parallel priority channel for the team."

## Scrum Master principle

Protecting the Sprint does not mean saying "no" to stakeholders. It means creating a transparent decision path.
