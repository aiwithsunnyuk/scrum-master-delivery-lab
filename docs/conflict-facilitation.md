# Conflict Facilitation

## Scenario

The Product Owner wants to prioritize end-to-end request status because of customer value.

The Engineering Lead wants to prioritize technical resilience work because recent environment instability creates delivery risk.

Both positions are reasonable.

## Scrum Master objective

Do not decide who is right.

Create a conversation where the parties can inspect evidence, constraints, risk and value.

## Facilitation sequence

### 1. Establish the shared objective

"What outcome are we both trying to protect?"

### 2. Separate positions from interests

**PO position:** Prioritize request status.

**Underlying interest:** Customer value and adoption.

**Engineering position:** Prioritize resilience.

**Underlying interest:** Sustainable delivery and reduced operational risk.

### 3. Inspect evidence

Review:

- Customer impact
- Production/operational risk
- Dependency status
- Cost of delay
- Product Goal alignment
- Technical risk

### 4. Make trade-offs explicit

Do not manufacture a false consensus.

### 5. Let the accountable people decide

Product priority remains with the Product Owner.

Technical implementation and engineering quality remain with Developers/engineering accountability.

### 6. Record the decision

Document the rationale and any follow-up.

## Coaching question

"What information would make this decision easier?"

This is often more useful than asking, "Who is right?"

## Anti-patterns

- Scrum Master becoming the tie-breaker.
- Escalating every disagreement to management.
- Avoiding conflict to preserve artificial harmony.
- Treating technical risk as separate from product value.
