# Sprint Metrics Review — Example

## Sprint 01 → Sprint 02

| Metric | Sprint 01 | Sprint 02 | Observation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Capacity days | 45 | 44 | Similar capacity |
| Forecast points | 20 | 18 | More conservative forecast |
| Completed points | 17 | 15 | Lower completed scope |
| Sprint Goal | Achieved | Achieved | Outcome remained successful |
| Velocity | 17 | 15 | Trend requires context |
| Median cycle time (days) | 3.2 | 3.8 | Flow slowed |
| Spillover points | 3 | 3 | Stable |
| Defects found after acceptance | 2 | 1 | Quality signal improved |
| High impediments | 1 | 1 | External dependency remained relevant |

## Interpretation

The most important result is that both Sprints achieved their Sprint Goals.

Sprint 02 completed fewer points, but the team also had an external dependency interruption. Cycle time increased modestly, so the team should inspect dependency and WIP effects rather than chase a higher velocity.

## Improvement hypothesis

**If critical dependencies are validated before Sprint Planning and work is sliced smaller, cycle time should become more stable.**

This hypothesis should be tested in the next Sprint rather than treated as proven fact.

## Scrum Master takeaway

Metrics are conversation starters. The Scrum Master helps the team inspect patterns and choose experiments. The Scrum Master should not manufacture a "good" metric.
